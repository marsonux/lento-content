Think of the task you've been putting off the longest.

How long would it actually take to do?

A lot of them take less than an hour. We rarely put something off for a week because it's too big. Far more often, it's because **getting started feels heavy**.

## Procrastination is about mood, not time

Many psychologists don't see procrastination as laziness or a failure of time management.

As researchers explain it, what we're avoiding is less the task itself and more **the unpleasant feeling of facing it**. And putting off something you don't want to do really does make you feel better in that moment.

The trouble is that this works like a painkiller. The feeling eases for a bit, but the task is still there, and next time it comes back a little heavier.

## 10 minutes isn't for finishing

So the goal of this habit isn't to finish. It's to **get through the hard part**.

The 10 minutes are for walking through that uncomfortable feeling. Once you open the file and start the first line, you find out firsthand that the pain you expected was bigger in your head.

And after 10 minutes, you really can stop.

**Knowing you're allowed to stop is what makes starting feel lighter.** The funny part is that once a task gets rolling, you often want to keep going.

## Try this today

**Pick just one**

Choose one task that's been nagging at you for days. It doesn't have to be the biggest one.

**Set a timer and just open it**

Set 10 minutes and don't try to do it well. Just open it. Opening the file, writing the first sentence, tearing open the box: that counts as starting.

**Decide after 10 minutes**

Whether to keep going or stop is up to you at that point. Even if you stop, you've done your part for today.

With a task you've put off, **most of the weight lifts the moment you start**, not when you finish.

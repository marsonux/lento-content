We read a huge amount every day.

We glance at headlines, check messages, scroll feeds, and skim search results.

But **reading something and understanding it are pretty different things**.

To understand what you read, you have to hold on to what came before, connect it to what comes next, and think about why the author said it. Some studies have linked the ability to stay focused while reading to how well you understand what you read.

So sometimes what you need isn't more reading. It's **time spent on one thing without wandering off**.

## Why 10 minutes is enough

There's no special science behind the number 10.

You don't have to decide to finish a book today. You just need **a stretch small enough to start focusing without pressure**.

Stay with one piece of writing for just 10 minutes.

If you hit a word you don't know, don't jump to another app right away. When a sentence catches your interest, slow down a little. Think about how it connects to what came before.

You may read less than usual.

But more of it may stick.

## Some people schedule time to think

At Microsoft, Bill Gates took what he called a "Think Week." He did it twice a year for decades.

For that week he stepped away from his usual schedule to read books and technical papers, think, and write. He cut out as many distractions as he could.

You don't need a whole Think Week.

But you can carve out **a 10-minute Think Time each day**.

What matters isn't how much you read. It's giving one idea your full attention, even for a little while.

## Try this today

Pick one book or article you've been meaning to read.

Then, for just 10 minutes:

**Read one thing**

Try not to jump to other articles or links.

**Pause once**

If a sentence catches you, stop and think about it for a moment.

**Write one line**

At the end, write a single sentence: "What I learned is ___."

You don't need to take in more new information every day.

**Truly understanding one thing a day.**

As that small difference adds up, you may slowly become less someone who knows a lot and more **someone with a lot to think with**.

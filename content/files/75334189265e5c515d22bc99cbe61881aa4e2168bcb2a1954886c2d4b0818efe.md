You can probably remember what you had for lunch yesterday, but not what you learned yesterday.

Still, you did learn something. A comment from a coworker, something you happened to read, a lesson that only landed after a mistake. A single day holds more learning than it seems.

The problem is that most of it **ends with the day, never written down anywhere**.

## It's the recall that saves it

We tend to think of writing things down as a backup against forgetting. Memory research tells a slightly different story.

Study after study has found that, given the same amount of time, **trying to recall what you learned keeps it in memory much longer than rereading it**. Psychologists call this "retrieval practice."

So this habit doesn't pay off in the sentences piling up in your notes. The payoff starts with **the few seconds before bed when you dig for an answer** to "What did I learn today?"

## Why just one sentence

Not a three-line summary or a set of notes. Just one sentence.

To boil it down to one sentence, you have to choose what's worth keeping from today and then put it in your own words. Those two steps turn someone else's information into **something that's yours**.

And once you've decided to save one thing a day, you start listening differently during the day. If you know you'll need something to write down tonight, the same meeting sounds different.

## Try this today

**Start with "Today I learned:"**

A set format makes it easier to begin. You can do it lying in bed.

**Write it from memory**

Don't look back at notes or messages yet. Get it down on your own first. This is the part that counts.

**Keep them all in one note**

A single note in your notes app is all you need. Just add the date.

A month gives you thirty sentences. That's just the right amount to read back in a few scrolls.

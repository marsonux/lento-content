Some things are at their best right before you buy them.

The moment it lands in your cart, you're already picturing yourself using it, and in that picture it looks a little better than it really is.

And checkout happens right when that picture is at its brightest.

## Wanting something has an expiration date

Think of something you really wanted to buy last month but never did.

Do you still want it that badly?

Some of the things you wanted back then, you probably can't even name anymore. The urge to have something cools faster than you'd think. Yet most of the time, **we check out before it cools.**

Waiting a day gives that urge some time.

If you still want it tomorrow, that's a pretty reliable sign. If it's lost its shine in a day, maybe what yesterday's you was trying to buy wasn't the thing but **the feeling of wanting to buy it**.

## Waiting isn't the same as resisting

This habit isn't asking you to spend less.

Cutting back says "Don't buy it." Waiting says "Ask tomorrow's you." You keep the decision. You just **hand it to a calmer version of yourself**.

That's why things you buy a day later often turn out to be more satisfying. You bought them because they passed a test, not on impulse.

## Try this today

When you find something you want, instead of checking out:

**Just save it**

Put it in your cart or wishlist and close the app.

**Look again in a day**

Open it at the same time tomorrow and check only whether you still want it.

**Still want it? Buy it**

If you waited a day, go ahead and buy it, guilt-free.

## How often this works best

You don't need to check every day. It's a rule that only kicks in when you want to buy something.

Instead, pick **one time each week to look through your wishlist**. Five minutes on a Sunday evening is plenty.

Buy what made it through the week, and delete what lost its shine. Each time you delete one, you also start to see the moments when you tend to waver.

Buying less isn't the point. What waiting leaves you with is **purchases you don't regret**.
